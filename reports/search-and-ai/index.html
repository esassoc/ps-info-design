Finding PS Info in Search

October 16, 2026

Google and Bing can reach every PS Info site, and the pages carry substantial, accurate content. What holds the site back is that most pages are hard for search engines to find and thinly described. None of the five sites publishes a page map, and nearly every page leaves its search summary blank.

Most of the fixes are small. They help AI assistants too, which find pages through the same search indexes.

How Search Engines Find a Page

  1. Discover

    Search engines follow plain links from page to page and read each site's page map. A page they can't reach this way may never appear in results.

  2. Understand

    They read the page's name, summary, main heading and text to decide what it's about and which searches it answers.

  3. Show

    The page name becomes the blue headline of the result and the summary becomes the text underneath. With no summary, the search engine picks text from the page itself.

    What's Already Working

    Open to every search engine

    Google, Bing and AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity all receive exactly the same page a visitor does. Nothing is blocked or challenged.

    Indicator findings are real text

    Descriptions, key results, targets and last-updated dates are written into the page itself, so search engines can read them without running any scripts.

    Substantial content

    Typical pages carry 700 to 2,500 words of real explanation. There are no thin placeholder pages on the Action Agenda, Vital Signs or Progress Indicators sites.

    Accessible and secure

    Every page scores 89 to 100 for accessibility, every site is secure, and every page declares its language.

    What Gets in the Way

    Being Found

    Whether a search engine can discover the page at all.

    The sites publish no map of their pages

    None of the PS Info sites (PS Info, Action Agenda, Vital Signs, Progress Indicators, NEP Atlas) offers the standard page list that search engines look for first, or the short file that tells them what they may read. They have to discover every page by wandering links.

    The indicator lists are invisible to anything that doesn't run scripts

    The Indicators list, the Progress Indicators list and the Vital Signs indicator lists draw themselves in the browser after the page loads. A search engine reading the page as delivered sees no links to the indicators. Following plain links, it reached none of the 86 indicators in the Indicators list and about 28 of the roughly 70 Vital Signs indicators.

    Search Console isn't set up

    No sign of Google Search Console or Bing Webmaster Tools on any of the sites. Without them there is no way to submit a page list, see which searches bring people in, or measure whether any of these changes worked.

    The short address doesn't work

    Typing pugetsoundinfo.wa.gov without the "www" goes nowhere. The address isn't connected to anything.

    Being Understood

    Whether a search engine can tell what a page is about once it arrives.

    Pages have no description

    186 of 189 pages have no summary for search results. Search engines then pick text themselves, usually from the navigation menu, which reads the same on every page.

    Page names are generic and repeated

    Names read like "Vital Signs | Orcas" or "Progress Indicators" with stray spaces, and every tab of an Action Agenda topic shares one name, so 84 of 86 Action Agenda pages have the same name as another page. An indicator page is called "PS Info | Urban growth", with no mention of Puget Sound or what kind of measure it is.

    Status and trend aren't written down

    Whether an indicator is getting better, meeting its target or showing mixed results appears only as an icon. The words exist only in the icon's hidden label, and charts are images or drawings with no data in text. Search engines have little readable text to match a question like "is eelgrass getting better".

    The same indicator lives at two addresses

    Eelgrass Area is published on both the PS Info site and the Vital Signs site, and nothing says which is the official one. The two copies split the credit in search results.

    No page has a main heading yet

    Every page shows its title as styled text rather than a true heading, so search engines lose the clearest signal of what the page is about. This is already fixed and goes live with the next release.

    Shared links have no preview

    A PS Info link pasted into email, Teams or social media shows no title card, summary or image. The sites also don't identify themselves as a Washington State agency resource in the way search engines can read.

    Being Trusted

    Signals that tell search engines the site is reliable and current.

    Missing pages look like site failures

    A removed or unknown indicator returns a server error rather than "not found". To a search engine that reads as a broken site, not a retired page.

    A handful of broken links

    Three Vital Signs links lead to errors, one points to the wrong site, and two links on the PS Info programs page are unfinished placeholders. Software demo pages bundled with the site are also publicly reachable on the official addresses.

    Link text says little

    Links on every page tested use labels that don't say where they go. On the Vital Signs home, the wheel's links are all labeled "Vital Sign link" rather than the name of each vital sign.

    Speed

    How long people wait, which search engines measure and weigh.

    Pages are very slow on phones

    On a typical phone connection the main content takes 20 to 51 seconds to appear (4 to 18 seconds on a desktop). Every page scores 52 to 55 out of 100 for speed on phones. The site sends its files uncompressed, makes browsers download them again on every visit, and loads editing tools that only signed-in staff use. The PS Info home page weighs 6.8 MB, most of it the embedded map story.

    Addendum: AI Assistants

    AI assistants that cite sources find pages through the same search indexes as everyone else: ChatGPT leans on Bing, Gemini on Google.

    What AI Assistants Cite Today

    Six questions a resident, reporter or partner might ask, put to ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity without signing in.

    QuestionChatGPTGeminiPerplexity

    Named topics win

    Ask about the Action Agenda, the Vital Signs or a specific indicator, and both ChatGPT and Gemini go straight to PS Info and repeat its numbers accurately, such as 51,700 acres of eelgrass.

    Broad questions go elsewhere

    Questions like how the Sound is doing or how vessels affect orcas are answered from State of the Sound, the Encyclopedia of Puget Sound and NOAA, even though PS Info covers both.

    Weak names travel with the citation

    Citations show the page names the site gives them: "2026 AA Explorer", "PS Info", or a bare web address. Those are the labels a reader sees next to the answer.

    Becoming the Source AI Cites

    Everything in the search plan helps here too. A few things matter even more for AI.

    Say the answer in a sentence

    AI quotes sentences, not icons or charts. A plain summary line at the top of each indicator stating its current value, target and direction is the most quotable thing PS Info could add.

    Be linked from the sites AI already trusts

    Broad answers come from State of the Sound, the Encyclopedia of Puget Sound, NOAA and Wikipedia. Links from those pages to the specific PS Info indicator make PS Info the source behind the source.

    One name and one home for each thing

    Consistent names like "Puget Sound Vital Signs" rather than "VS" or "AA Explorer", and one address per indicator, so every mention builds the same page's reputation.

    Show that it's current

    A visible last-updated date and reporting period tell people and AI that the figure is recent enough to repeat.
