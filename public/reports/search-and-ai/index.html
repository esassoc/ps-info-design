1Summary

This report assesses how well search engines and AI assistants can find, interpret and present the PS Info sites, identifies the issues that limit their visibility in search, and recommends a sequence of changes.

All sites are open to search engine and AI crawlers, and their pages carry substantial, accurate content. Visibility is limited by four conditions:

  • Discovery. No site publishes an XML sitemap or a robots.txt file, and the indicator lists are rendered in the browser by script. A crawler following links reached none of the 86 indicators in the PS Info Indicators list and about 28 of the roughly 70 Vital Signs indicators.
  • Description. 186 of 189 pages have no meta description. Page titles are generic, and 84 of 86 Action Agenda pages share a title with another page.
  • Performance. On a simulated mobile connection, the main content of the pages tested takes 20 to 51 seconds to render.
  • Measurement. Neither Google Search Console nor Bing Webmaster Tools is configured, so search traffic and the effect of any change cannot currently be measured.

Most Phase 1 changes in Section 7 are small and independent of one another. Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools should be configured first, because they establish the baseline against which every later change is measured. The same changes improve how AI assistants cite PS Info, since those assistants retrieve pages through the same search indexes (Appendix A).

3How Search Engines Index a Page

Search engines handle a page in three stages. Each finding in Section 6 affects one of them.

  1. Discovery. Crawlers find pages by following plain HTML links and by reading each site's sitemap. A page that cannot be reached either way may never be indexed.
  2. Interpretation. The crawler reads the page title, meta description, main heading and body text to determine what the page covers and which searches it answers.
  3. Presentation. The page title becomes the headline of the search result and the meta description becomes the text beneath it. Where no description exists, the search engine selects text from the page, often from the navigation menu.

5Existing Strengths

  • Open to crawlers. Google, Bing and the crawlers used by ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity receive the same page a visitor does. No crawler is blocked or challenged.
  • Indicator content is server-rendered. Descriptions, key results, targets and last-updated dates are present in the delivered HTML and can be read without running scripts.
  • Substantial content. Typical pages carry 700 to 2,500 words. The Action Agenda, Vital Signs and Progress Indicators sites have no thin placeholder pages.
  • Secure and well formed. Every site is served over HTTPS, and every page declares its language.

6Findings

6.1Discovery

No sitemap or robots.txt on any site

None of the five sites publishes an XML sitemap, the list of pages search engines read first, or a robots.txt file, which states what crawlers may read and where the sitemap is. Crawlers must discover every page by following links.

Indicator lists are rendered by script

The PS Info Indicators list, the Progress Indicators list and the Vital Signs indicator lists are built in the browser after the page loads. The HTML a crawler receives contains no links to the indicators. Following plain links, a crawler reached none of the 86 indicators in the PS Info Indicators list and about 28 of the roughly 70 Vital Signs indicators.

Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools are not configured

No site shows verification for either service. Without them, sitemaps cannot be submitted, the searches that bring visitors to PS Info cannot be seen, and the effect of the changes in Section 7 cannot be measured.

The address without "www" does not resolve

pugetsoundinfo.wa.gov, without "www", is not connected to any site and returns no page.

6.2Page Metadata and Content

Meta descriptions are missing

186 of 189 pages have no meta description. Search engines fill the result with text they select, usually from the navigation menu, which is identical on every page.

Page titles are generic and duplicated

Titles take forms such as "Vital Signs | Orcas" or "Progress Indicators", some with stray spaces. Every tab of an Action Agenda topic shares one title, so 84 of 86 Action Agenda pages share a title with another page. An indicator page is titled "PS Info | Urban growth", with no reference to Puget Sound or to the kind of measure it is.

Status and trend are not present as text

Whether an indicator is improving, meeting its target or showing mixed results is shown only as an icon; the wording exists only in the icon's hidden label. Charts are images or drawings with no data in text. A search such as "is eelgrass getting better" has little readable text to match.

Indicators are published at two addresses

Eelgrass Area is published on both the PS Info site and the Vital Signs site, and neither page declares a canonical address. The two copies divide ranking signals between them.

No page has an h1 heading

Page titles are displayed as styled text rather than an h1 element, removing the strongest on-page signal of what a page covers. This is corrected in the current codebase and goes live with the next release.

No link previews or structured data

Pages carry no Open Graph tags, so a PS Info link shared in email, Teams or social media shows no title, summary or image. No page identifies the site as a Washington State agency resource in structured data.

6.3Reliability Signals

Missing pages return a server error

A removed or unknown indicator returns a server error rather than 404 Not Found. Search engines read this as a fault in the site, not a retired page.

Broken and placeholder links

Three Vital Signs links lead to errors, one points to the wrong site, and two links on the PS Info programs page are unfinished placeholders. Software demonstration pages bundled with the platform are publicly reachable on the production addresses.

Link text does not describe the destination

Every page tested has links whose labels do not state where they lead. On the Vital Signs home page, every link in the vital signs wheel is labeled "Vital Sign link" rather than with the name of the vital sign.

6.4Performance

Slow rendering on mobile

On a simulated mobile connection, the main content of the pages tested takes 20 to 51 seconds to render (Largest Contentful Paint), and 4 to 18 seconds on desktop. Every page tested scores 52 to 55 out of 100 for mobile performance. Contributing causes:

  • Files are served without compression.
  • Caching headers require browsers to download the same files on every visit.
  • Editing tools used only by signed-in staff load for every public visitor.
  • The PS Info home page weighs 6.8 MB, most of it the embedded map story.

AAppendix: AI Assistants

AI assistants that cite sources retrieve pages through the same search indexes: ChatGPT relies on Bing, and Gemini on Google. The recommendations in Section 7 therefore apply to AI citation as well.

A.1Citations by Question

Each question was put to ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity without signing in. Perplexity required sign-in after the first question. Cells list the PS Info page cited, or the leading sources cited instead.

QuestionChatGPTGeminiPerplexity

    Three patterns appear in the results:

    • Named topics are cited. Questions that name the Action Agenda, the Vital Signs or a specific indicator lead ChatGPT and Gemini to PS Info, and both repeat its figures accurately, such as 51,700 acres of eelgrass.
    • Broad questions are answered elsewhere. Questions on the overall health of the Sound or on vessel effects on orcas are answered from State of the Sound, the Encyclopedia of Puget Sound and NOAA, although PS Info covers both topics.
    • Page titles appear in the citation. Citations show the titles the site assigns: "2026 AA Explorer", "PS Info", or a bare web address.

    A.2Factors That Affect Citation

    Beyond the changes in Section 7, four factors weigh more heavily for AI assistants than for search results:

    • A summary sentence on each indicator. AI assistants quote sentences, not icons or charts. A sentence at the top of each indicator stating its current value, target and direction is the most quotable addition available to PS Info.
    • Links from sources AI assistants already cite. Broad answers draw on State of the Sound, the Encyclopedia of Puget Sound, NOAA and Wikipedia. Links from those pages to specific PS Info indicators would establish PS Info as the underlying source.
    • Consistent names and one address per item. "Puget Sound Vital Signs" rather than "VS", and "Action Agenda Explorer" rather than "AA Explorer", with one address per indicator, so that every mention accrues to the same page.
    • Visible currency. A last-updated date and reporting period on each indicator indicate that the figure is current enough to repeat.

    An example of a quotable sentence already on PS Info:

    "Eelgrass covers about 51,700 acres of Puget Sound (2021 to 2023 average)."

    The recommendation is a sentence of this form, including status and trend, on every indicator.
